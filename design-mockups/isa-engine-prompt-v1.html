04 The arithmetic spine — populated

v0.9 specified the structure of extent computation but no numbers. These parameters are the engine defaults (per-firm overridable in sampling_config, every override logged):

4.1 MUS — tests of details

Sampling interval = Tolerable misstatement ÷ Confidence factor
Sample size       = Population to be tested ÷ Sampling interval
Population to be tested EXCLUDES high-value and key items (examined 100%, evaluated separately).
Random start in [1, interval]; cumulative monetary stepping; selection is value-proportional.
Required confidenceFactorRisk-reduction band
99% · 98% · 95%4.6 · 3.7 · 3.0High = 95% (significant/elevated risk, no controls reliance)
90% · 85% · 80%2.3 · 1.9 · 1.6Moderate = 80–90% (some reliance from controls or analytics)
75% · 70% · 65%1.4 · 1.2 · 1.1Low = 65–75% (substantial assurance from other sources)
60% · 55% · 50%0.9 · 0.8 · 0.7Minimal — corroborative only
  • Stratification supported by monetary band or characteristic (e.g. ageing bucket for AVA testing).
  • Projection: per-item misstatement percentage → averaged across all sampled items → applied to the represented population = most likely error (MLE). MLE + known misstatements compared to tolerable (R18).
  • Anomaly (ISA 530.13): claiming a misstatement is non-representative requires additional procedures establishing a high degree of certainty, and approval — never a default.

06 The derivation algorithm

v0.9's thesis — "programmes are derived, not stored" — now specified. The programme for an account is a function of its relevant assertions, their risk tier, and the library:

for account in significant_accounts:
  for assertion in relevant_assertions(account):            # ISA 315 A188
    tier = risk_tier(account, assertion)                    # baseline | heightened | significant
    steps = library.query(cycle=account.cycle,
                          assertions CONTAINS assertion,
                          risk_level_applicability <= tier)  # tiers are cumulative
    if assertion.significant_risk:
        assert steps.any(nature == 'test_of_details')       # R06 — else block G2
    if strategy(account) == 'controls_reliance':
        steps += control_tests(account, assertion)          # attribute plan from §4.2
    programme[account] += dedupe(steps)
programme is REGENERATED (not edited) whenever tier, strategy or library changes;
completed steps that survive regeneration keep their state; removed steps archive;
new steps enter as not-started. Manual additions persist as 'firm-added', flagged to review.

6.1 SCOT identification, understanding & walkthrough

The derivation loop above starts from a documented understanding of each significant class of transactions (SCOT) — not from the account balance alone. That understanding is a first-class, gated record per ISA 315 (Revised 2019) ¶25–26, A128–A136:

ElementRequirement
IdentificationEvery transaction flow feeding a significant account is classified routine (recurring, systematic — sales, purchases, payroll), non-routine (unusual by size or nature — acquisitions, restructurings) or estimation (measurement uncertainty — allowances, provisions, fair values). A flow is a SCOT when it feeds a significant account or carries an identified RMM. Coverage: every significant flow-driven account maps to ≥1 SCOT; balance-only accounts map to a period-end process instead.
Understanding record (one per SCOT)The critical path documented step by step — initiation → recording → processing → reporting — naming at each step the actors and their segregation, the documents and records produced, the IT applications touched (each application auto-enters Annex F ITGC scope), the IPE generated, and the control points. Each what-can-go-wrong is raised per assertion at the step where it arises and answered by a control (→ test it, §4.2) or a substantive step (→ derive it, §06 loop).
WalkthroughUnderstanding is confirmed, never assumed: one transaction traced end-to-end through the documented path each period, corroborating inquiry with observation and inspection at every step. The walkthrough record holds the item traced, the evidence per step, and deviations. A deviation, or any change to the flow, marks the SCOT understanding STALE — its WCGWs, linked risks and derived programme steps reopen (R05 propagation).
Data modelNew scot entity: id · name · type (routine|nonroutine|estimation) · accounts[] · applications[] · steps (critical-path jsonb) · walkthrough_status · walkthrough_date. The risk entity's flow_ref references scot.id; wcgw_text holds the what-can-go-wrong sentence.
Gate wiringG2 gains two conditions: every significant account maps to a SCOT (or period-end process) with a complete understanding record, and every SCOT carries a current-period walkthrough. R33 reports the orphans continuously: SCOTs without risks, significant accounts without SCOTs, WCGWs without a control or substantive answer.
AuditISA deltaThe E1 group (Significant Transaction Classes) already models the methodology as work steps inside one task per flow — understanding → walkthrough → controls → substantive. The scot entity backs those steps: the understanding record and walkthrough are the task's evidence, and Annex F application scoping reads from scot.applications.

Annex F · IT overlay

  • Six risks of using IT (the risk taxonomy for the IT section of planning): reliance on inaccurate systems or data · unauthorised data access · IT-personnel privileges breaking segregation · unauthorised master-file changes · unauthorised program/system changes · failure to make necessary changes.

Annex J · Estimates overlay (ISA 540 (Revised))

  • Every accounting estimate is rated for estimation uncertainty at planning; high uncertainty → auto-significant (R09) with its R06 consequences (test of details required).
  • The response tests one or more of: how management made the estimate — method, significant assumptions and data each evaluated; an auditor's point estimate or range — the range confined to outcomes supported by evidence; or events to the report date that resolve the estimate.
  • The retrospective review of prior-year estimates (locked in by R07) informs the bias assessment; bias indicators accumulate to the fraud evaluation (R22 candidate). Management's point outside the auditor's range → judgemental misstatement, measured to the nearest range edge, onto the schedule (B5/R18 evaluation).
  • Standback: estimation-uncertainty disclosures evaluated (P assertion); skepticism documented — corroborating and contradicting evidence both addressed.
  • Data model: estimate-linked risk rows gain uncertainty_rating and method/assumption/data test flags. Annex procedures X.130 · X.150 · Q.140 · F.140 route here.
