# IAASB Fact Sheet — Introduction to ISA 315 (Revised 2019)

> Working digest of the IAASB "Introduction to ISA 315 (Revised 2019)" fact sheet (December 2019, 5 pp.).
> Its page-3 flow diagram is the canonical picture of the revised risk-assessment process.

## Why the revision

Revised for **clarity, consistency and scalability**: requirements say *what*, reorganized application
material says *why* and *how*. Outcomes sought: consistent application, principles-based scalability,
less complexity, **more robust risk assessment → more focused responses**, modernized guidance (IT,
automated tools). Effective for periods beginning on or after 15 December 2021. **The audit risk model
itself did not change.**

## The process diagram (page 3)

Vertical spine, top to bottom, wrapped by "Professional Judgment and Professional Skepticism",
"Documentation" on the right, and the reminder that risk assessment is **dynamic and iterative,
continuing throughout the audit**:

1. **Risk assessment procedures** — fed by acceptance/continuance information and by prior-audit
   experience; the **engagement team discussion** sits alongside.
2. **Obtain an understanding of**: the entity's **system of internal control** (control environment,
   risk assessment process, monitoring | information system & communication, control activities —
   emitting **control deficiencies**) and the **entity & its environment** plus the **applicable
   financial reporting framework** — emitting **inherent risk factors**.
3. **Identify RMM** at the **financial-statement level** and the **assertion level**, iterating with
   **determine significant classes of transactions, account balances and disclosures and relevant
   assertions**.
4. **Assess**: FS-level risks; **inherent risk at assertion level**; determine risks where
   **substantive procedures alone are insufficient**; determine **significant risks**; then the fork —
   **does the auditor plan to test operating effectiveness of controls?** Yes → **assess control
   risk**; No → **RMM = inherent risk assessment**.
5. **Stand-back**: for material classes/balances/disclosures not determined significant — does that
   remain appropriate?
6. Outputs: assessed RMM at FS level and assertion level → **audit responses (ISA 330)**, with
   **revision of risk assessment** looping back.

## Significant changes called out

- Enhanced professional-skepticism requirements; risk assessment explicitly the basis for ISA 330 design;
  separate focus on understanding the framework.
- System of internal control: direct vs indirect controls distinguished; which controls need
  design-evaluation + implementation-determination clarified.
- **New concepts**: *significant classes/balances/disclosures and relevant assertions*; *spectrum of
  inherent risk* (likelihood × magnitude judgment on a range); *inherent risk factors* (complexity,
  subjectivity, change, uncertainty, management bias/fraud).
- **Separate assessment of inherent and control risk**; significant risk redefined via the upper end of
  the spectrum; **no controls testing planned → RMM = inherent risk**.
- **New stand-back** on completeness of significant classes; modernized IT material (new appendices);
  strengthened documentation of skepticism; scalability guidance under dedicated headings; automated
  tools & techniques woven through.
