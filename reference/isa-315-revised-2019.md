# ISA 315 (Revised 2019) — Identifying and Assessing the Risks of Material Misstatement

> Working digest for AuditISA development, condensed from the IAASB Final Pronouncement (December 2019, 202 pp.).
> Effective for periods beginning on or after 15 December 2021. The PDF is the authority; paragraph
> references (¶ for requirements, A for application material) point back to it.

## 1. Key concepts (¶1–10)

- RMM exists at **two levels**: the financial-statement level (pervasive) and the assertion level
  (classes of transactions, account balances, disclosures).
- Assertion-level RMM has two components, now assessed **separately**: **inherent risk** (susceptibility
  before controls) and **control risk** (the system fails to prevent/detect/correct in time).
- Inherent risk varies along the **"spectrum of inherent risk"** — the degree matters, not just a flag.
- The process is **iterative and dynamic**: understanding, framework and controls interdepend; initial
  expectations get refined; assessments are revised as evidence arrives (with ISA 330).
- **Scalability**: one standard for all entities; complexity, not size, drives depth.

## 2. Definitions worth engineering around (¶12)

| Term | Essence |
|---|---|
| Business risk (b) | Risk from conditions, events, actions/inactions that threaten the entity's objectives and strategies — or from bad objectives/strategies themselves |
| Inherent risk factors (f) | Characteristics driving susceptibility to misstatement: **complexity, subjectivity, change, uncertainty, management bias / fraud susceptibility** |
| Relevant assertion (h) | An assertion **with an identified RMM** — determined before controls |
| Significant class/balance/disclosure (k) | One with **at least one relevant assertion** |
| Significant risk (l) | RMM assessed **close to the upper end of the spectrum** (likelihood × magnitude), or mandated by another ISA (ISA 240 ¶27, ISA 550 ¶18) |
| System of internal control (m) | Five components: control environment; entity's risk assessment process; monitoring; information system & communication; control activities |
| General IT controls / IT environment (d, g) | Controls over IT processes (access, change, operations) supporting information-processing controls and information integrity |

## 3. Requirements map (¶13–38)

### Risk assessment procedures (¶13–18)
- ¶13 Design/perform RAPs giving an appropriate basis for identification/assessment and for ISA 330 design — **unbiased** (not skewed to corroborating or excluding contradiction).
- ¶14 RAPs must include **inquiries** (management, others, internal audit), **analytical procedures**, **observation & inspection**.
- ¶15–16 Consider acceptance/continuance information and other engagements; evaluate whether prior-period information remains relevant/reliable.
- ¶17–18 **Engagement team discussion** on framework application and susceptibility to misstatement; brief absentees.

### Understanding (¶19–27)
- ¶19 Understand: (a) entity & environment — organisational structure, ownership, governance, **business model incl. IT integration**; industry/regulatory/external factors; **performance measures** used internally/externally; (b) **framework and accounting policies** and reasons for changes; (c) **how inherent risk factors affect susceptibility** of assertions.
- ¶20 Evaluate whether accounting policies are appropriate and framework-consistent.
- ¶21 **Control environment**: understand + **evaluate** (culture/ethics; TCWG independence & oversight; authority/responsibility; competence; accountability; foundation adequacy; deficiencies undermine the rest).
- ¶22 **Entity's risk assessment process**: how management identifies business risks relevant to reporting, assesses significance/likelihood, addresses them + **evaluate appropriateness**.
- ¶23 If the auditor finds RMM **management's process missed**: determine why the process failed and what that means for ¶22(b).
- ¶24 **Monitoring** (incl. internal audit) + information sources + evaluate.
- ¶25 **Information system & communication**: flows from initiation to reporting, records, FR process, IT resources; communication internal / with TCWG / external + evaluate.
- ¶26 **Control activities — identify only**: (i) controls over significant risks; (ii) **journal-entry controls**; (iii) controls the auditor plans to test (incl. where substantive alone insufficient); (iv) others by judgment. From those, identify IT applications subject to risks arising from IT → the risks and **GITCs** addressing them. For every identified control: **evaluate design + determine implementation** (inquiry alone not enough).
- ¶27 Determine **control deficiencies**.

### Identifying & assessing (¶28–37)
- ¶28–29 Identify RMM at FS and assertion levels; determine **relevant assertions** and **significant classes/balances/disclosures**.
- ¶30 FS-level risks: do they affect assertion assessments; evaluate pervasiveness.
- ¶31 Assess **inherent risk by likelihood × magnitude**, weighing inherent risk factors and FS-level effects.
- ¶32 Determine **significant risks** (upper end of spectrum).
- ¶33 Determine risks where **substantive procedures alone can't suffice** (highly automated flows, integrated IT).
- ¶34 **Control risk**: assess only if planning to test operating effectiveness; **otherwise RMM = inherent risk**.
- ¶35 Evaluate whether RAP evidence provides an appropriate basis — considering **corroborative and contradictory** evidence; do more if not.
- ¶36 **Stand-back**: for material classes/balances/disclosures NOT determined significant, evaluate whether that determination remains appropriate.
- ¶37 **Revise** the assessment when new inconsistent information arrives.

### Documentation (¶38)
Discussion + decisions; key elements & sources of understanding; RAPs performed; control design/implementation evaluations; RMMs at both levels with **rationale for significant judgments** (incl. evidence of skepticism, A238).

## 4. Application-material highlights

- **A7–A8** Inherent risk factors qualitative (complexity, subjectivity, change, uncertainty, bias) and quantitative (size/volume/uniformity).
- **A61–A65 + Appendix 1 (business model)**: understanding objectives, strategies and the business model reveals the business risks the entity takes and faces; **"most business risks will eventually have financial consequences and, therefore, an effect on the financial statements."** The auditor need not identify *all* business risks — only those that may yield RMM. Sources of such risks: inappropriate objectives/strategies, ineffective execution, change/complexity, failure to recognize the need for change, incentives/pressures → bias. Example matters (A64): industry developments, new products (liability), expansion vs demand, new accounting requirements, regulatory exposure, financing constraints, IT change, strategy side-effects.
- **A68–A81**: industry/regulatory/external factors; performance measures create pressure (KPIs, budgets, incentive pay, covenants, analyst expectations).
- **A190 assertion categories**: transactions — occurrence, completeness, accuracy, cutoff, classification, presentation; balances — existence, rights & obligations, completeness, accuracy/valuation/allocation, classification, presentation.
- **A193–A200 FS-level risks**: pervasive circumstances (going-concern doubt, management-integrity concerns, poor control environment, records reliability) → overall responses; may make an audit impossible (ISA 705).
- **A202–A204**: relevant assertions scope the information-system understanding; automated techniques can surface significant classes (e.g., zero-balance clearing accounts with heavy traffic); qualitative disclosures may be significant (liquidity/covenants, impairments, estimation uncertainty, related parties).
- **A205–A214 spectrum**: significance = intersection of likelihood and magnitude (either can dominate; high magnitude/low likelihood can still be higher-spectrum); auditors **may designate categories along the spectrum** (a rating scale is compliant).
- **A218–A221 significant risks**: consequences — controls over them must be identified and D&I-evaluated; ISA 330 mandates testing controls (if relying) and specifically responsive substantives; more persuasive evidence; TCWG communication; KAM candidates; partner review; ISA 600 attention. Typical drivers: multiple acceptable treatments, high-uncertainty estimates, complex data collection or calculations, contested principles, business changes (M&A).
- **A224 substantive-alone-insufficient**: highly automated, little manual intervention, evidence electronic-only (telecom revenue example).
- **A226–A229 control risk**: initial CR expectation from design + implementation; confirm by ISA 330 testing; revise if ineffective.
- **A233–A236**: materiality vs significance stand-back; revision triggers.
- **A237–A241 documentation scalability**: less complex entity → simpler, possibly folded into strategy/plan docs; key elements suffice; skepticism evidenced.

## 5. Appendices (1–6)

1. **Business model** — scope of activities, structure/scale, markets & value chain, processes (investment/financing/operating), resources, IT integration; special-purpose entities.
2. **Inherent risk factors** — full narrative on the five factors + an events/conditions table per factor (complexity: complex regulation, alliances/JVs, off-balance-sheet finance; subjectivity: wide measurement criteria, valuation-technique choice; change: economic instability, volatile markets, going-concern/liquidity strain, industry/supply-chain/product change, expansion, acquisitions, key-personnel departure, IT change, new pronouncements, capital constraints, investigations; uncertainty: estimates, litigation, contingencies; bias/fraud: disclosure-obscuring opportunities, related-party transactions, non-routine period-end items, intent-based accounting) + FS-level indicators (skills shortfall in reporting, control-environment deficiencies, past misstatements/heavy period-end adjustments).
3. **System of internal control** — five components elaborated: control-environment elements; entity's risk assessment process (change circumstances: operating environment, new personnel, new/revamped IS, rapid growth, new technology, new business models, restructurings, foreign expansion, new pronouncements, IT dependence); monitoring vs information-system controls; information system scope; control-activity types (authorizations, reconciliations, verifications, physical/logical, segregation of duties — with small-entity alternatives).
4. **Internal audit** considerations.
5. **IT** considerations (applications, infrastructure, processes; when IT risk is higher).
6. **GITC** considerations (access, change, operations, by IT layer).
